Setting Up Out-of-Office and Focus Time in Google Calendar

Google Calendar’s Out of office event does something no regular event does: it declines meetings for you, automatically, with your words. Used well it also covers focus days, part-time schedules, and appointment-only hours. Here is the full mechanics, the decline-message wording that keeps goodwill, and the setups nobody documents.

6 min read · Updated Jul 6, 2026 · Written by Sergey Ilyin

What the Out of office event type actually does

Create an event → choose Out of office (on the event-type row) and the event changes species: it renders as a solid block, and — the headline feature — automatically declines every invitation that overlaps it, existing and future, with a message you set. Colleagues browsing your calendar see the block; schedulers get an instant, polite no without you ever seeing the invite.

Two behaviors worth knowing before you rely on it: the auto-decline fires immediately on creation for meetings already on the books (a heads-up in your team channel spares the confusion), and the event blocks the entire span visually — this is by design, since it signals "don’t plan around gaps".

The decline message is the whole product

The default text is serviceable; a good one carries three facts — you’re out, when you’re back, who covers what:

"I’m out until Monday, Jan 19, with no calendar access. For release questions: Dana. Anything else keeps until I’m back — please re-invite me for the following week if it needs a meeting."

The last clause quietly does the rescheduling for you: people re-book into your return week instead of waiting for you to untangle a pile of declined threads.

Setups beyond vacation

The focus day that defends itself

A monthly deep-work day gains teeth as an Out of office block titled honestly: "Focus day — auto-declining; Slack me if urgent". Unlike a normal busy block, it cannot be booked over by someone who didn’t look. Use sparingly — one day a month reads as discipline, every Friday reads as unavailable-as-identity (Workspace’s separate Focus time type, with optional auto-decline, is the daily-use tool).

Part-time and compressed weeks

Working four days? A recurring Out of office every Friday makes the schedule self-enforcing: nobody needs to remember, because the calendar declines for you. Set the recurrence exactly like any event (weekly, on Friday), write a message that names your working days, and the pattern teaches itself to the org within two weeks.

Guarding the edges of a trip

Start the OOO at noon the day before travel and end it at noon the day after return. The half-days absorb packing and re-entry — and prevent the classic 9:00-the-morning-back meeting that makes vacations feel like they end early.

What Out of office does not do

It does not answer email (pair it with Gmail’s vacation responder — the two are separate systems), it does not cascade to recurring meetings you organize (delegate or cancel those yourself), and it does not show a reason to free/busy viewers — they see busy, not "on leave", so external clients may still email expecting same-day replies. The Gmail responder covers that last gap.

Re-entry is the underrated half of time off: the first morning back is a wall of what moved while you were out. A toolbar view like Calendar Extension for Google Calendar™ turns that first check into a day strip you can absorb with coffee in hand — what’s today, what’s next, what got re-booked into your return week.
