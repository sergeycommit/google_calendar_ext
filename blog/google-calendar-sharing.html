Google Calendar Sharing and Permissions Explained

Calendar sharing has four permission levels, and most sharing problems come from picking the wrong one — usually one level too generous. Here is what each level actually exposes, the exact setup for the three most common situations, and the mistakes that leak private detail.

6 min read · Updated Jul 6, 2026 · Written by Sergey Ilyin

The four permission levels, translated

LevelThey can seeRight for
See only free/busyBlocks of "busy" — no titles, no guests, no locationsCross-team scheduling, external partners
See all event detailsTitles, guests, descriptions, attachments — everything not marked PrivateYour team, your manager
Make changes to eventsAll details, plus create/edit/delete rightsAn assistant who manages your day
Make changes and manage sharingEverything, including who else gets accessAlmost nobody — this is ownership in all but name

The rule that prevents most regret: start one level lower than feels natural. Upgrading someone later is one click; discovering a contractor has been reading your interview loop titles for a month is not reversible.

Setup for the three common cases

Share with a specific person

In the left sidebar, hover the calendar → three-dot menu → Settings and sharing → Share with specific people or groups → Add people. Pick the person and the permission level. They get an email and the calendar appears in their sidebar under "Other calendars".

Share with your whole team

Same screen, but add a Google Group address instead of individuals. Membership then manages itself — people joining the group inherit access, leavers lose it. Sharing with ten individuals is how orphaned access happens after every reorg.

Open availability to an entire Workspace org

In Settings and sharing → Access permissions for events, tick "Make available for [your organization]" and choose See only free/busy. This is the setting that makes "find a time" work for colleagues without exposing a single event title.

The two-layer trap: calendar vs. event privacy

Sharing is layered, and the layers interact in one direction only. A calendar shared with "See all event details" still hides any event whose own visibility is set to Private (event → visibility dropdown). Use this for the handful of genuinely sensitive entries — medical, interviews, comp conversations — instead of downgrading the whole calendar to free/busy and breaking your team’s context.

Three mistakes that leak detail

  1. Sharing the primary calendar when a purpose-built one would do. If a client only needs to see project milestones, create a "Milestones" calendar and share that. Your primary calendar is your whole life; slices of it rarely need an audience.
  2. "Make changes" as a courtesy. Edit rights get granted because the dialog is open and it feels collegial. Every person with edit rights is someone whose mis-drag becomes your missed meeting.
  3. Forgetting that descriptions travel. "See all event details" includes descriptions and attachments — the doc link, the dial-in PIN, the candidate feedback pasted into the invite. If the calendar is broadly shared, the invite body is a broadcast channel.

Key takeaways

  • Share purpose-built calendars, not your primary one, with external audiences.
  • Org-wide free/busy is what makes "find a time" work without exposing titles.
