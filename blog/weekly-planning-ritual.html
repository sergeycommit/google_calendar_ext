Building a Weekly Planning Ritual That Takes Under 20 Minutes

Weekly planning has a credibility problem: the hour-long Sunday ceremony produces a beautiful plan that dies by Tuesday. The version that survives is 20 minutes, runs on three questions, and treats the plan as starting conditions rather than prophecy. Here is the ritual, minute by minute, and the drift-checks between weeks.

6 min read · Updated Jul 12, 2026 · Written by Sergey Ilyin

Three questions, not twelve steps

Strip every weekly-planning system to its load-bearing walls and three questions remain: What happened? (evidence), What matters next week? (selection), Where does it live on the calendar? (commitment). Everything else — the color-coded life audit, the values re-alignment, the goal pyramid — is optional ceremony, and ceremony is what gets skipped under load, taking the walls down with it.

Minutes 0–6 · Evidence: read last week like a report

Open last week’s calendar and read it as data, not as diary. Three tallies, rough is fine: meeting hours (rising or stable?), focus blocks planned vs. held (the credibility ratio), and where the plan diverged (which day collapsed, and what did the collapsing?). The credibility ratio is the number to watch across weeks — planning more focus than you historically hold is how plans lose authority with their own author.

Minutes 6–12 · Selection: the two-rock rule

Name what next week must produce: two outcomes, phrased as deliverables ("pricing proposal reviewed by team", not "work on pricing"). Two, because rocks get protected calendar blocks, and the evidence pass just told you how many blocks you can actually hold. Everything else that clamors gets a fair trial for the shallow-batch slots or an honest deferral — a deferral decided Friday costs nothing; the same deferral discovered Thursday costs a fire drill.

Minutes 12–18 · Commitment: put it on the grid

Now the calendar work, in priority order: two rock-blocks in your best hours (estimate +25%, marked Busy, titled by output); buffers repaired after next week’s worst meeting clusters; fictions deleted (the hold that never confirmed, the optional you won’t attend — decline tonight, with notice); and one line of instruction in Monday’s first block. If the rocks don’t fit, the ritual has done its most valuable work early: renegotiate now — decline something, shrink something, or downgrade a rock — while every option is still cheap.

Minutes 18–20 · The handshake

Close with the plan’s terms of service, said once to yourself: the rocks are commitments, the rest is weather. This framing is what separates planning from prophecy — when Tuesday explodes (it will), the question is only "do the rocks survive?", not "is the plan dead?". A plan that expects turbulence doesn’t capsize in it.

Drift-checks between rituals

Two micro-habits keep consecutive weeks honest. The Wednesday pulse (60 seconds): are both rocks still alive? If one is dying, decide its fate mid-week — rescue, shrink, or officially defer — instead of letting Friday’s evidence pass find a corpse. The broken-ritual rule: a skipped week gets the 5-minute version (selection + rock placement only) on Monday morning, never a guilt-powered double session. Rituals survive on cheap re-entry.

The evidence pass and the fiction-hunt are both scanning work — faster on a glance surface than in the full grid. Anchors, yes: the school pickup, the gym slots, the dinner — they are load-bearing constraints and go on the grid first. Aspirations, no: "be more present" is not schedulable and its failure poisons the plan’s credibility. The calendar plans commitments; values live upstream of it.

Fix the plan first: plan half the blocks you want, hold them, and rebuild trust with yourself over three weeks. A third-held ratio usually means over-planning (too many rocks), under-defending (blocks marked Free), or both — and both are plan-side settings, not character flaws.

Key takeaways

  • Three questions: what happened, what matters, where does it live.
  • Two rocks maximum — the evidence pass tells you what you can actually hold.
  • Fictions deleted Friday cost nothing; discovered Thursday they cost fire drills.
  • Rocks are commitments, the rest is weather — that handshake is the whole philosophy.
