How to Schedule Across Time Zones Without the Chaos

Timezone scheduling chaos is not a math problem — the arithmetic is trivial — it is a fairness-and-defaults problem: whose 7:00 absorbs the mismatch, and does the calendar make the cost visible before booking. Here is the Google Calendar setup, the fairness patterns that survive, and the DST traps.

6 min read · Updated Jul 6, 2026 · Written by Sergey Ilyin

Make the calendar do the arithmetic

Four settings turn Google Calendar into a timezone instrument:

  • Secondary timezone (Settings → Time zone → display secondary): your grid gains a second hour-column — Berlin left, New York right — and every slot reads in both without mental math. Label them ("Me" / "NYC team").
  • World clock (Settings → World clock): pick your collaborators’ cities; the sidebar shows their now, including a subtle day-boundary warning when it’s already tomorrow there.
  • Per-event timezones: the event editor’s time-zone link pins an event to a zone — essential for travel ("board meeting, 14:00 Singapore") so the event moves correctly with your device instead of drifting.
  • Working hours, everyone, honestly: the whole fairness machinery below runs on colleagues’ published hours being true. An unset working-hours field is a silent vote for being scheduled at 6:00.

The fairness patterns (this is the actual problem)

Any recurring cross-zone meeting sits on someone’s edge. Three patterns distribute the pain deliberately instead of by seniority-gravity:

  1. Fixed-but-compensated: the meeting stays at the overlap’s least-bad slot permanently (predictability wins), and the edge-carriers get visible compensation — the 18:00-holders start late those days, on the calendar. The compensation being scheduled is what keeps it real.
  2. Seasonal rotation: the slot alternates quarterly, not weekly — "whose 7:00 is it this quarter" beats weekly roulette, because bodies adapt to stable schedules and never to rotating ones.
  3. The split double: past ~7 hours of spread, one live meeting punishes someone structurally. Run the same agenda twice (EU edition / US edition) with a shared doc as the spine, or go async-first with a fortnightly all-zones call at the least-bad hour. A meeting nobody is asleep for beats a "unified" one that a third of the team attends as zombies.

The three DST traps

Daylight saving is where timezone scheduling actually breaks, because zones shift on different dates. Trap one: the US springs forward two-three weeks before Europe — every EU↔US recurring meeting silently moves an hour relative to one side during the gap windows (mid-March and late October–early November: audit those weeks explicitly). Trap two: events created in the wrong zone anchor: a "9:00 call" created while traveling anchors to the travel zone forever — per-event timezones are the fix. Trap three: zones without DST (most of Asia, parts of Australia offset by half-hours) mean your "stable" 16:00 Singapore sync moves for you twice a year while never moving for them. Calendar handles the math correctly in all three cases; the trap is human expectations, so the audit is a calendar-week scan, not a formula.

Etiquette across the date line

Three courtesies that cost nothing: state zones explicitly in every proposal ("Thu 15:00 CET / 9:00 ET" — naked times start wars), respect the working-hours warning as a stop sign (ask, don’t book: "this lands at your 19:00 — okay once, or alternate?"), and put your own travel on the calendar with working location so the team’s arithmetic about you stays current.

Cross-zone days breed a specific glance: "what time is this call for me, and how long until it?" A toolbar strip with countdown chips — Calendar Extension for Google Calendar™ — answers in your local frame automatically, which on split-zone days is worth more than any mental conversion habit.

Frequently asked questions

The classic corridor is 15:00–17:00 CET / 9:00–11:00 ET — morning-fresh for the US, late-but-inside-hours for Europe. The equitable move is not finding a "perfect" slot (none exists) but keeping the corridor’s load balanced: EU takes the 17:00s, US takes the occasional 8:00, visibly.

Accept that no live slot includes everyone humanely: go async-first (docs and threads as the primary medium), hold zone-pair syncs in their natural corridors, and reserve the all-hands for a fortnightly rotating-least-bad slot with recordings as first-class citizens. The triple-zone "daily standup" is a hazing ritual, not a process.

Recurring events anchor to their creation timezone: when DST shifts asymmetrically (the March/October gap windows), one side moves relative to the other by design. Check the series’ timezone in the editor — anchoring it to the zone that hosts the meeting’s constraint (the client’s, the majority’s) is the deliberate choice.

Key takeaways

  • Secondary timezone + world clock + honest working hours = the instrument panel.
  • Fairness is designed: compensate the edge-carriers, rotate quarterly, or split past 7 hours.
  • Audit the DST gap windows (mid-March, late Oct) — zones shift on different dates.
  • State both zones in every proposal; naked times start wars.
